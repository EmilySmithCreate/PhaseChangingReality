# Reading note: published mathematics for energy crossing from three dimensions to two (6 November 2026, night)

**Why:** her question of 6 November, night: "the crossing term from three dimensions to two - is there any
available physics math research that explores this?", after her caution of the same night that the ledger's map to
MeV "is not simply additive and the energy changing from 3d to 2d maybe has an additional term at play". **Who:**
an assistant session, from memory; every item below is general knowledge, **to verify at the source within the
week** (rule 19); nothing read in full tonight. The answer: yes, in at least four published forms, and one is the
standard mathematics of exactly her phrase "curled where ours are open".

## What the toy already says (exact)

The flat baseline differs between the phases: on the four-link sheet every link closes two squares, in the six-link
cube four. So the same curl, one surplus square, sits against different baselines, and one unit of the ledger is
not the same energy in the two phases. A map to MeV needs one conversion per phase or a term for the crossing.
That is the toy's own version of the question; it says nothing about which published form fits.

## Published forms, nearest first

1. **Kaluza-Klein reduction** (Kaluza 1921; Klein 1926; any textbook on extra dimensions). A theory in more
   dimensions, with one dimension curled into a small circle, looks from the lower dimension like a theory with
   extra terms: a field's motion round the curled direction appears in the lower dimension as **mass**, in steps
   set by the circle's size (the Kaluza-Klein tower, m_n = n / R), and the metric's components along the curled
   direction appear as new fields (a gauge field and a scalar). This is the published mathematics of a curled
   direction, and it says exactly what she suspected: crossing from the higher dimension to the lower adds a term,
   and that term is a mass. For her picture: energy held along the curled direction of X is mass in the opened
   daughter. Nearest of all; to be read first.
2. **Dimensional reduction in quantum gravity** ('t Hooft 1993, "Dimensional reduction in quantum gravity",
   gr-qc/9310026; the holographic principle): the degrees of freedom of a region of three-dimensional space can be
   counted on its two-dimensional boundary, with the entropy bound S = A / 4 in Planck units (Bekenstein 1973;
   Hawking 1975). The count of what a volume holds is a term on its surface. Her inner shell as two-dimensional,
   and her target 15 (one bit per 4 ln 2 Planck areas, `docs/design/reality_targets.md`), are this.
3. **Spontaneous dimensional reduction at short distance** (Ambjorn, Jurkiewicz and Loll 2005, the spectral
   dimension of causal dynamical triangulations running from 4 to 2; Carlip's reviews: "Dimension and dimensional
   reduction in quantum gravity", arXiv:1705.05417 (2017), and the one checked at source tonight, *Universe* 5(3),
   83 (2019), arXiv:1904.04379, doi:10.3390/universe5030083; the "Class. Quantum Grav. 34, 193001" citation written
   from memory earlier tonight was not confirmed and is withdrawn): in several approaches to quantum
   gravity the effective dimension of spacetime falls to two at the smallest scales. The published model's own
   author takes the fundamental degrees of freedom to be two-dimensional (his advice on paper 1), which is this
   family. For her: the two-dimensional phase as the small-scale truth and three dimensions as what emerges.
4. **Compactification energy, the Casimir energy of a curled direction** (the energy a field has because one
   direction is a small circle; Appelquist and Chodos 1983 for Kaluza-Klein): the vacuum energy depends on the
   circle's size, which is a term that exists only because the direction is curled and changes as it opens. For
   her: the burp's release as the change of this term when the curled direction opens.

## What to take from them, and what not

The form to borrow is 1: the crossing term is a mass term, set by the size of the curled direction. The toy's
version would be: a quantity held along the tube's four-around direction counts, after the opening, as a mass in
the sheet. That is a structure choice (ours) once written as a rule; the number it gives is not a result until the
size of the curled direction has a source. Not to be taken: that the toy's phases are related by a Kaluza-Klein
reduction in the technical sense (a smooth higher-dimensional field theory); the toy is a lattice of squares and
cubes, and the correspondence is of shape only until an exact check is made.
