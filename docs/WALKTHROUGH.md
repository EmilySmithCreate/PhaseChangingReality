# A Phase-Changing Reality, in plain English

As of 6 November 2026. Written for a reader with no physics background. The drawings are in the Claude document of
the same name and in `sandbox/drawings/2026-11-06_rod_arrangements.html`; this file is the text's home in the
repository. Drafted with Claude (Anthropic) in conversation with Emily Smith; the hypothesis is hers.

## The idea in one breath

Our universe was born inside a black hole, when a tightly curled arrangement of dark matter gave way and released
its energy all at once. That release was the Big Bang. The space we live in is the opened-up version of that curled
thing. Inside our own black holes the same curling happens again, so every black hole can give birth to new
universes, and the whole pattern repeats.

A few words you will meet. **X** is the name for the curled arrangement before it opens. **The burp** is the moment
it gives way. **Curl** is a measure of how tightly a bit of the arrangement is wound; **twist** is a handedness,
left or right, that sits on top of curl. **A daughter** is a universe born from a burp. **The toy** is the computer
model the ideas are tested on.

## What X is

Start inside a black hole. Everything that falls in is crushed, and in Emily's picture what gets crushed hardest is
dark matter: the invisible stuff that outweighs ordinary matter five to one and that gravity feels but light passes
through. Under enough pressure the dark matter lines up, all of one handedness, and winds itself into a tight flat
sheet. That sheet is X.

A sheet with no edges that is made of little squares has a shape it must take: a doughnut. (That is a piece of
ordinary geometry, not a guess: a closed surface built only of squares, four at every corner, is always a doughnut,
never a ball.) So X is a doughnut of wound-up dark matter. Through its hole runs the rod, where dark matter is still
spiralling in from outside. Where the spirals reach the doughnut's inner rim they can go no further and lock in
place. Those locked pieces are called Klein stores, and they hold energy the way a wound spring does. Each store is
two pieces of dark matter bound into one loop, and Emily's working number is that the bond holds about a proton's
worth of energy, thousands of times the mass of the two pieces themselves; that number is a calibration against
the proton, not a prediction.

Nothing moves inside the doughnut: no light, no ordinary matter, no time in the usual sense. Only dark matter and
gravity act there. That stillness is why it can store so much.

## The burp: how a universe is born

A black hole keeps eating. Everything that falls in adds energy to the doughnut, and the doughnut cannot pass it on,
so the energy piles up in the locked stores. There is a limit to how much a wound spring can hold. When the stores
pass that limit, the winding lets go. The release is sudden and total, like a cork leaving a bottle, and the energy
that comes out is the energy of a Big Bang. Emily calls it the burp.

The burp does not blow the black hole apart. It opens a new space *inside* it, a daughter universe that grows into
room that did not exist before. From the parent's side the black hole is unchanged. The daughter starts as a hot
flash of light, cools, and ends up with stars, galaxies and, eventually, black holes of its own. Those crush dark
matter into new doughnuts, and the loop closes.

Two things stay fixed from one turn to the next: energy is never lost, only moved into the daughter; and the rules
do not change, so every universe in the tree runs on the same physics as ours.

## Why there is more matter than antimatter

This is the oldest puzzle in cosmology. The Big Bang made matter and antimatter in equal amounts, and when the two
meet they cancel into light. If they had been exactly equal, nothing would be left: no atoms, no stars, no us. In
fact about one particle in a billion survived, and everything we can see is that leftover. Physics has no agreed
explanation for the imbalance.

Emily's answer is about *where* the burp happens. The last burp leaves a loose patch at the doughnut's inner rim.
The next burp starts there, because loose is easiest to open. Its seed straddles the patch's edge like someone
standing with a foot on each side of a line. The foot inside the patch stops; the foot outside grows into the
wound-up part and becomes the daughter universe.

Matter and antimatter are, in her picture, the two handednesses of twist, left and right. Her rule is that the
first pair made across the edge decides which hand goes outward, and every pair after follows it. When a left and
a right half meet and cancel, what leaves as light is their twist; the curl they sat on stays behind as a neutral
dark object with no twist, which she calls a dark gluon, the shape of the neutral pion in ordinary physics. So the daughter
is born carrying one hand, and the patch that stays behind carries the other. Over the whole black hole the books
balance; inside any one daughter they do not, and that is the surplus we are made of. The published idea nearest to
this is the CPT-symmetric universe of Boyle, Finn and Turok (2018), where our universe and an anti-universe sit on
the two sides of the Big Bang and cancel as a pair.

What this does not yet explain is the *size* of the leftover. Her rule fixes which hand wins, not how little
survives. One in a billion is the number any version of this picture must eventually produce, and it has not
produced it.

## The rest of the picture, briefly

**Dark matter.** A single loop of twist that has only one side, like a Möbius strip, with half a turn of handedness
on it. Because it has one side it cannot carry electric charge, so light ignores it, but gravity does not. It is the
building material of X. Ordinary particles are loops with two sides and whole turns; the electron is the smallest,
and its mass is the unit everything else is measured in.

**Gravity.** Space has its own urge to curve; matter supplies the energy that starts the curving rather than causing
it. In the model, every link in the network has a length, and a link's length is set by how much curl sits on it.
The attraction half of gravity has been shown this way. The time half, clocks running slow near mass, has not, and
that is an open problem.

**Dark energy.** Empty space grows on its own, by whole rows at a time, only where it is empty. So the universe's
expansion is slow while matter is clumping and speeds up as empty space takes over, which is roughly what
astronomers see.

**Time.** Time exists because things move through open space. Inside the doughnut nothing moves, so there is no
time there, only an order. Each daughter universe runs its own clock from its own birth; no clock runs across
universes.

**The quantum world.** Her reading is the many-worlds one, with the standard rule that lets versions interfere and
a record in the surroundings that keeps them apart. This part is adopted from published physics, not derived.

## What has actually been tested

The ideas are tested on a toy: a computer model of a network of points joined by links, whose rule is a published
model of how geometry can emerge from a network (Trugenberger's), plus one extra cost for winding tight. A toy can
show that a mechanism is possible or impossible within a class of models. It can never show that the real universe
works this way. Every test is written down before it runs, with Emily's prediction in her own words and one size
held back to check the claim on, and every result is kept whether it helps or not.

What has held, as of 6 November 2026:

- A wound-up network really does give way all at once, releasing a fixed amount of energy, and the push that starts
  it costs the same at every size. That is the burp, and it is the oldest and firmest result.
- A sheet packed with energy forms locked stores of both hands, more of them the tighter the winding, as she
  predicted.
- On a tube with one loose patch, the opening starts at the patch, twenty to sixty times sooner than anywhere else,
  and the seed straddles the patch's edge with a foot on each side, at every size tried (test T107, this week).
- Under her rule for which hand goes outward, the two feet come out as mirror images at every size, the sign
  matching in 54 of 57 cases where there was anything to read.
- Two knots of curl pull toward each other, and light's path past a knot is longer, as her gravity needs.

What has failed or is missing:

- The size of the matter surplus. The toy gives about one part in fifty to one in a hundred, falling as the model
  gets bigger; the sky says one in a billion. Whether the trend reaches it is the next test.
- One of this week's predictions, that the outer foot is larger than the inner one at a fixed time, failed at the
  largest size, because the inner foot's size is set by the patch and the patch grows with the model. It is being
  rewritten in terms of growth.
- The time half of gravity: the model's own clock runs the wrong way near mass.
- The locked stores do form from energy alone, two same-hand pieces joining wherever there is no room (T105),
  but the network itself has never curled from energy: the wound-up X is built by hand, and the squeeze of gravity
  that would do the winding in her picture is not built on the sheet yet.
- Nothing quantum has come out of the model; that part is adopted.

No physicist has reviewed any of this yet.
