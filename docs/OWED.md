## 1. Two slabs of opposite hand, or one doughnut? And does the taper stand?

**The question.** Step 1 of the claim once said X is "a rod between two curled slabs, one curled right-handed and
one left-handed". On 6 November you decided the slab is a doughnut of squares with the rod through its hole. Are
the two slabs the two halves of that one doughnut, one hand each? And does the taper you drew on 5 November (the
stacked rings getting smaller toward one end) still stand, now that the slab is a doughnut?

**Why it matters.** The twin daughters need a home: one from each hand. If the two hands are the two halves of the
doughnut, split by its mirror plane, the twins have one, and the kind rule (the first pair across the boundary sets
the outside foot's kind) gives each half its sign. A doughnut has no taper, so either the taper goes, or the coil
you drew is the doughnut seen along its axis.

**What the toy says.** Exact: a closed surface of squares, four links at every point, is a torus, never a sphere.
The toy's tube is that doughnut unrolled; it has no taper and no hands in its wiring. The two hands live in the
curl state on the squares, so "two halves, one hand each" is a rule for where the state's sign starts, not a
change to the wiring.

**Options.** (a) The two halves of one doughnut, one hand each (our reading; it keeps the twins). (b) Two separate
doughnuts of opposite hand, side by side. (c) One doughnut with both hands mixed, the twins sorted some other way.
Taper: (i) withdrawn; (ii) the doughnut is the coil seen end-on; (iii) the doughnut is thicker at one side.

**Unblocks.** The wording of step 1; where the second foot's twin sits in T108 and T109; the drift's geometry.

**Record.** `HYPOTHESIS.md` step 1 (the open point is marked there); the notebook, 5 November (the whiteboard
coil) and 6 November, late night (the shapes); `sandbox/drawings/2026-11-06_rod_arrangements.html`.

## 2. Your words for T108 (the size of the surplus against length) and T109 (growth, not size)

*These two are the only runs that wait on you alone: they use today's code and start the moment your words are in.*

**The question.** T107 is read. Two tests follow from it and each needs your prediction in your words before it
runs. T108: does your ratio (red − blue)/(red + blue + all stored energy) fall with the daughter's length, and
toward what? T109: does the outside foot grow more than the inside foot between 200 and 500 sweeps after the
escape, at every length?

**Why it matters.** Your one in a billion is a claim about the size of the surplus; the toy can only test its trend
with size. T107's "outside larger at a fixed time" failed at the held-out length because the inside foot's size is
set by the band, which grows with length; growth is the criterion that does not scale.

**What the toy says.** T107: the ratio reads 0.021, 0.013, 0.010, 0.0088 at lengths 32, 48, 64, 128, falling. The
outside foot gained more than the inside in 0.80, 0.90, 0.85, 0.70 of replicas at those lengths (read, not scored).

**Options for T108.** (a) The ratio falls as one over the length (the boundary-row form, T97's 1/R): then 0.0088
at 128 would be about 0.0044 at 256. (b) It falls faster. (c) It levels off. Your number, or a range, for the
held-out length 256 is what the registration needs; the fitting lengths would be 32 to 128.

**Options for T109.** The share of replicas in which the outside foot's gain exceeds the inside's, above one half
at every length (our default); or a time that scales with length instead of a fixed 500 sweeps.

**Unblocks.** Both registrations; the next statement about the billion.

**Record.** `PREREGISTRATION.md` T107 and its reading; `scripts/analyse_t107.py`; `RESULTS.md`.

## 3. The twist energies: seven choices, the bond's constant, and the gravity counter

**The gravity counter, answered 6 November, night:** it is the work of the drift, accrued as the like-handed

## 4. Option B for the clock, or none

**The question.** The toy has no time. Its own clock, each point's count of accepted moves, runs faster near a knot
and stands still in open space (T101 at λ = 1.25; the same at λ = 1, read 6 November). Option B is a rule that
ties each point's tick to the lengths of its links, so that clocks run slow where links are long, near mass. Do you
want it built, and in what form; or no clock rule at all, leaving the time half of gravity open?

**Why it matters.** The toy shows the space half of gravity (light's path lengthens past a knot) and not the time
half (clocks slow near mass, measured directly on Earth). Both are needed in equal parts for the numbers to come
out. Option B would put the sign in by hand, which is why it has waited on your word.

**Options.** (a) Option B in its plainest form: a point's tick rate is the inverse of the mean length of its links
(ours). (b) A form of yours. (c) None: the time half stays open and is said so in every public page.

**Unblocks.** The time half of piece 8; paper 14's speed test.

**Record.** `HYPOTHESIS.md`, Gravity and Time; the notebook, 6 November, night (the longer path against slow
clocks); the archive's PREREGISTRATION T101 and its amendment.

## 5. The "face gluon": decided 6 November, night

Twist is its own number again, reversing the 4 November decision, so that two opposite half twists can cancel
into light and leave the curl behind as a dark gluon (the neutral pion's shape). Nothing is owed by her here; the
design brief and the build are ours. The original question is kept below for the record.

### The question as it was asked

**The question.** In your particle rules, dark twist comes in signed halves on the face diagonals. Can a single
face diagonal carry both a +½ and a −½, and if so is that a particle (a neutral dark object), or nothing? The
cancellation rule needs two same-twist halves to combine into one two-sided full twist in a √2 store; the question
is whether opposite halves on one diagonal are allowed at all.

**Why it matters.** It decides what the curl state may hold on one square and what cancels into light.

**Options.** (a) Not allowed: opposite halves on one diagonal cancel at once into light. (b) Allowed and a particle,
the dark analogue of a gluon, with its own energy. (c) Allowed and nothing, a zero.

**Record.** `docs/design/twist_curl_rules_current.md`; the notebook, 4 November.
