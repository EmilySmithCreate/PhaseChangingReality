# A Phase-Changing Reality: a reader's guide

As of 6 November 2026. Written for a friend who wants to read the work with an AI assistant and, if he likes,
send pull requests. A shareable, exportable copy of this page lives as a Claude document; this file is its home

## What this is

This is a reader's guide to Emily Smith's hypothesis that our spacetime is one settled arrangement of something
deeper, called X, which gave way in a sharp change, the burp, that released the energy of the Big Bang; and to
the toy model on which its pieces are tested.

The hypothesis is Emily's. The toy, the tests and all the writing, this guide included, were drafted with Claude
(Anthropic) in conversation with her. Every statement in the record says which of four kinds it is: **hers** (a
claim about reality, stated to be argued with), **exact** (arithmetic or a complete enumeration), **measured** (a
registered test on the toy with its verdict), or **ours** (a reading by the assistant, unverified by any
physicist). A toy can show that a mechanism is possible or impossible within a class of models. It can never show
anything about the real universe; that claim is hers.

## The claim in short

The claim is hers, in ten steps; the full wording with its status marks is `HYPOTHESIS.md` in the working copy.
In one paragraph each:

1. **X is stable for now.** Our spacetime is one settled arrangement of something deeper, X, with its own
   dimensions, curled where ours are open. X is made of tightly lined-up dark matter: many one-sided loops of one
   handedness spiralling in from all sides of a sphere of squares, with the rod the line their stopping points
   make through the centre (her correction of 6 November). Underneath, the
   base has two degrees of freedom, curl and twist; points and links are a way of describing the
   three-dimensional space that emerges from them.
2. **Energy arrives, and the curl gives way: the burp.** A black hole's infall raises X's excitation. When the
   stores exceed the barrier the first curl snaps, a fixed release of energy, and the second follows. The burp
   is in the rod, not at a pole.
3. **The burp becomes the Big Bang's hot lump.** Light, with matter and antimatter in almost equal amounts;
   nearly every pair cancels into more light. Each burp makes twin daughters, one from each curled direction,
   one with a small surplus of matter and the other the matching surplus of antimatter, so the black hole stays
   neutral. The matter we are made of is that excess, about one part in a billion. A particle is a closed loop of
   twisted diagonals; the electron is the unit.
4. **The release leaves one way, and X re-forms.** Nothing passes from open space back into X; the black hole is
   a converter, not a store.
5. **What did not convert is the scrap.** Dark matter is a one-sided loop of surplus curl on the face diagonals:
   it meets gravity and not light, and it is the heaviest particle.
6. **Dark energy is the energy of space itself.** Empty space grows at no cost, by whole rows, on a steady clock,
   only where space is empty.
7. **The books balance, and the rules hold.** Energy is conserved across the change; curl and uncurl total zero;
   the constants do not change from one generation to the next.
8. **Black holes curl space back, and each births many daughters.** Gravity is carried on the cube edges of open
   space as a length on every link, set by its own curl. Inside a black hole, surplus dark matter is crushed into
   the next X. The tree of universes multiplies each generation.
9. **Each universe keeps its own clock.** No clock runs across universes.
10. **The quantum world is versions not yet told apart.** The versions are the branches of the many-worlds
    reading, with the published rule for amplitudes and records.

What distinguishes her picture from its published neighbors, in her words: the black hole as a converter whose
burps come in twin daughters of opposite surplus; dark matter as the material of X itself; and gravity as space's
own drive to curve, with matter supplying the energy that starts it.

## The toy model

The toy is a network of points of two colors, black and white, each with four links (six in the three-direction
version) joining black to white, with no two points sharing more than two neighbors. Its energy is a published
model of emergent geometry, Trugenberger's combinatorial quantum gravity, which rewards small square loops and
penalizes a link that carries too many, plus one cost of ours for curling tight, with strength lambda minus 1. At
lambda equal to 1 the toy is the published model; away from it, it is our family around that model. Flat space is
a torus in which every link closes its share of squares. X's stand-in is the tube: the torus with one direction
curled four around, which waits with no memory and then opens in one change, giving off exactly the curling cost
per point. That opening is the toy's burp.

On top of the wiring sit her two kinds: one real number per square, curl on the black-to-black diagonal (matter)
and anti-curl on the white-to-white diagonal (antimatter). The wiring holds them always paired, one of each per
square; the energy cannot tell them apart; a surplus of one kind lives only in that state. Pairs are made, cancel
into light where there is room, or join into Klein stores where there is none.

What the toy has shown, each a registered test with its number in `RESULTS.md`: the tube opens at a fixed push
of 12 units at every size (T6 to T13, the first paper); a daughter space forms per curling cost in the
three-direction slab (T71, T74); a window with her stored matter in it reads differently from empty space under a
published reader of geometry from entanglement (T94, T98); two knots' joint energy falls as they approach and
light's path past a knot lengthens, gravity's attraction half (T84); Klein stores form on a packed sheet and rise
with lambda (T105). What it has not shown: any mechanism that curls flat space from energy alone, the time half
of gravity, or anything quantum.

## How to read it with an AI

Point the assistant at the working copy and have it read `CLAUDE.md` first: that file is the assistant's own
instructions, and it fixes the reading order so that nothing already tested and set aside comes back as a new
idea.

1. `CLAUDE.md`: the rules and the reading order.
2. `HYPOTHESIS.md`: the claim as it stands, in her words, and the current definitions.
3. `WITHDRAWN.md`: what was tried and set aside. An assistant must never propose, assume or "remind" her of
   anything on it; if it thinks a withdrawn idea should come back, it says so once, as a question.
4. `STATE.md`: where each piece stands, what is running, what is owed, what is next.
5. `RESULTS.md`: every registered test and its verdict, one line each.
6. `TOY.md`: the toy term by term, and the code map.
7. `GLOSSARY.md`: her words, the standard terms, and words not to use.

The archive is for looking up a detail, a number, a quotation or a derivation; it is not for deciding what the
hypothesis is. If the two disagree, the working copy is newer.

The rules that matter most for a reader. Every assumption has a source or is marked ours. A published number is
reproduced before anything on that code is interpreted. Predictions are written down before the runs, in her
words. Results are append-only. A close match to a published number is not a result until it was derived
independently, its input uncertainty propagated, and the other combinations that would match counted. Every
objection names its kind: a convention (free to change), a measurement (her rules must reproduce the number), or
a theory's structure (replaceable by anything that gives the same numbers). Only a measurement may stop an idea,
and only after the arithmetic has been done.

Things that come in two opposite kinds, which an assistant has wrongly denied more than once: point color (black
and white); curl and anti-curl (the two same-color diagonals of one square); twist handedness (signed by the
color of the diagonal); matter and antimatter (the two colors of twist, made as equal pairs by rule); the twin
daughters of one burp; the two curled slabs of X. The right sentence is "the energy cannot tell the two kinds
apart", never "nothing here comes in opposites".

Two words of naming. Away from lambda equal to 1 the toy is "our family around the published model", never
"combinatorial quantum gravity"; that is the published model's author's condition. And "programme" with that
spelling is her joke, kept on purpose.

## Open questions and how to contribute

The open questions she is working on now, each with the exact test that would bear on it:

- **What sets the size of the surplus.** Her rule sets its sign; the size is a few twists out of 128 pairs in
  the toy against one in a billion in the sky. T107 reads her ratio at each length without scoring it (0.021,
  0.013, 0.010 and 0.009 at 32, 48, 64 and 128, falling with length); the registered test of the trend is next.
- **The drift of the halves before they join.** Decided 5 November, not built. Her answer of 6 November gives it
  a stopping rule: the halves stop where room runs out, the start of the Klein store layer. The design brief is
  `docs/design/drift_2026-11-05.md`.
- **The bond's strength.** The form is borrowed from 't Hooft's two-dimensional model (energy growing in a
  straight line with separation); its one constant has no value yet, and the form is exact for a line, not a
  plane.
- **Which carries the dark matter at birth**, the seam's rings or the knots that form later.
- **The time half of gravity.** The toy's lengths give the attraction; its own clock runs the wrong way near a
  knot (T101). A clock rule tied to the lengths would put the sign in by hand; its form is hers, or none.

Pull requests are welcome on the working copy. What a contribution needs, from `CLAUDE.md`: an idea starts in
the notebook (`sandbox/NOTEBOOK.md`) in the proposer's words with the date; a quick look goes in `sandbox/` with
one line in `sandbox/LOG.md`; a registered test has its prediction in `PREREGISTRATION.md` before it runs, its
config in `configs/`, its script in `scripts/`, and its one-line verdict in `RESULTS.md`; tests pass (`pytest`)
before every commit; a new kernel comes with a brute-force check, an exactness check of its incremental updates,
constraint-preservation checks and a same-seed check. Nothing on `WITHDRAWN.md` is proposed again without saying
so.
