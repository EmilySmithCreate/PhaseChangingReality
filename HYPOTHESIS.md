### 1. X is stable for now.

Our spacetime is one settled arrangement of something deeper, X: a specific, relatively stable arrangement with
its own dimensions, curled where ours are open.

X was stable for now, not for ever. It is a curled slab of tightly lined-up dark matter: many one-sided loops of
the same handedness, wound into a closed two-dimensional sheet of squares. A closed sheet of squares with four
links at every point is a torus, never a sphere (exact), so the slab is a doughnut. Through the doughnut's hole
runs the rod, three-dimensional, where dark matter still moves, spiralling in from all sides as one continuous
line, the two hands turning opposite ways. In the doughnut twist and curl are set and only dark matter and
gravity move; matter and photons cannot get through it. The whirlpool region outside the doughnut tapers: its stacked rings get smaller
toward the doughnut and the tapering finishes there, the funnel feeding the doughnut at its tip (decided
6 November, night; the doughnut itself has no taper). Dark matter comes in from both poles, two funnels, both
hands at each; the top half of the doughnut holds a majority of one hand and the bottom half of the other, not
purity, the hands arranged in rows; the two curled slabs of the earlier wording are these two halves, and the
twin daughters come one from each (decided 6 November, night; the simple form adopted as feasible, her rows kept
as what the toy is expected to show inside it).

The rod is not a thing at the centre of the slab; it is the hole's axis. The centre of the black hole is the set
of Klein stores sitting together on the doughnut's inner rim, where the spiralling dark matter can no longer move,
the tip of the curled slab: curl and twist set, zero degrees of freedom, much bond energy. Outside the doughnut
is three-dimensional spacetime, matter still moving but ever more slowly, until at the rim it stops.

The two curls are always made together, and curling two directions costs the same as curling one.

Underneath, the base has two degrees of freedom, curl and twist; points and links are a way of describing the
three-dimensional space that emerges from them. Three dimensions are a projection from the two-dimensional
arrangement, the result of the burp's energy wiring; the toy's flat sheet and its opened tubes are the toy's
stand-ins and end states, not stages of the change.

Curl is continuous, from −1 to 1, its two signs matter and antimatter at equal size. Twist is discrete: thirds,
which combine into a whole twist across several diagonals and meet light only at whole values, and halves,
which make one-sided matter, dark matter, the building blocks of the Klein bottle. The Klein bottle stores more
by combining two same-direction twists into one twist and one curl of length 1. Only a full curl, a loop,
stores energy stably.

### 2. Energy arrives, and the curl gives way: the burp.

Energy arriving in X, as a black hole's infall arrives, raises its excitation. Photons cannot get through the
curled slab: in the whirlpools outside it every kind of energy becomes dark matter and gravity, and gravity carries
the dark matter in along the square edges to the rim, where two like-handed pieces bind into one Klein store
heavier than its parts, the extra mass paid by gravity, which is one-directional and keeps adding. The bond has
the form of 't Hooft's two-dimensional model: its energy grows in a straight line with the separation of the two
halves, so they never come free.

When the stores exceed the barrier, the first curl snaps: the burp, a fixed release of energy. It happens in the
rod, at the rim between the rod and the doughnut, not at a pole: at a reflection seam, a Klein bottle holding two
dark-matter rings whose binding energy is released. The hold-back drops at once and the second curl follows, a
cascade.

The opening front is held back by a neutral layer at its edge, like a membrane, curl facing in and uncurl
facing out; its tension is why the curl needs a surplus to open. The burp's cycle leaves a loose remnant at the
rim, lower in density than the layers round it, and the next burp starts there, because loose is easiest to open.

### 3. The burp becomes the Big Bang's hot lump.

The release is the hot lump: light, with matter and antimatter in almost equal amounts. As it cooled, nearly
every pair of matter and antimatter cancelled into more light, so light carried almost all of the energy.

The burp's seed straddles the edge of the loose remnant with a foot on each side. The inside foot stops at the
remnant; the outside foot grows outward through the doughnut and becomes the daughter: one daughter is one foot,
a new three-dimensional spacetime born in the space the stores enclose. The first pair made across the boundary
sets the outside foot's kind, and every pair after follows it; the inside foot carries the opposite kind, the one
the previous burp's outside foot carried, so successive burps alternate. Then a gap, then another burp when enough
curl has built up again; they keep opening as each new two-dimensional space encloses a new centre to open a
spacetime into.

Each burp makes twin daughters, one from each hand, alike except that one has a small surplus of matter and the
other the matching surplus of antimatter; each gets half the curl energy, and the black hole stays neutral. The
release is fast, a far bigger explosion than any bomb.

The matter we are made of is that small excess: (red − blue) / (red + blue + all stored energy), about one part
in a billion of the squares in the daughter, the stores' barrier energy counted in. Matter to antimatter is one
to one by rule; the surplus is the stores' inside layer turned to curl, a count of stores against pairs, and
there are other factors at play. Every daughter calls her own surplus matter.

While the light has nowhere to go it is pressed back into matter, and matter is curved space: tiny loops that
are only a relationship, no points, metastable, which build quarks and then protons; once there is room, the
light leaves instead.

A particle is a closed loop of twisted diagonals: matter twist in signed thirds on the box diagonals, dark
twist in signed halves on the face diagonals, the sign the colour of the diagonal. What is whole is the loop's
net twist, a whole number of thirds summed round the loop; charge needs two sides and is carried only on
thirds. The loop's coil is a shape, not a charge. Mass counts the looped diagonals, twisted or not. Spin and
curl are precise: full spin on half a curl, the photon; half spin on a full curl, matter; spin needs twist
present, so the neutrino, whose twists cancel, spins. The electron, the neutron, the photon and the charged
pion all come out with their measured charge and spin. The proton is a large closed ring of about 5,500
diagonals, charged and spinning. The unit: the loop of three thirds of one colour, net twist −1, is the
electron, 0.511 MeV, charge −1, spin one half.

Matter and antimatter are the two colours of twist. A daughter keeps the twists whose partners are in her
twin, so each has a surplus; a black and a white twist that meet cancel into two photons.

### 4. The release leaves one way, and X re-forms.

The release goes into the twin daughters, one from each curled direction; nothing passes from open space back
into X. The slab re-forms and goes on accepting energy, so the black hole is a converter, not a store.

### 5. What did not convert is the scrap.

In each daughter, what the handedness pull of the shell held back from converting: energy beyond the curling
cost, stored by the force of the other pole of the curled slab. In the toy, small knots of fixed energy, made
while the new space is still hot enough to make flaws, then fixed.

Dark matter and dark antimatter are the face-diagonal strands: a one-sided loop of surplus curl, a flaw left
when space opened, and its mirror image. Charge needs two sides and rides only on thirds, so dark matter carries
none; it meets gravity and not light. The neutrino is two-sided, two half-twist loops of opposite hand. Whether
the toy's scrap is this dark matter is open.

The dark-matter particle holds the most energy and mass of any particle: it can take no more curl or spin, and
it meets gravity more strongly and light not at all, since light travels the body diagonals and not the edges.

### 6. Dark energy is the energy of space itself.

A candidate source, adopted as a hypothesis to test: the network's own thermal defect density, the few defects
any network carries at a coupling above zero. The coupling is the loop's, set by the loop's total energy once
every daughter's energy has returned to the network at the end of a turn.

Empty space grows at no cost, by whole rows, on a steady clock, and only where space is empty. The overall rate
slows while matter clumps and speeds up as empty space takes over; observers are likeliest in between.

### 7. The books balance, and the rules hold.

Energy is conserved across the change. Curl and uncurl always total zero: a region curled one way carries as
much uncurl somewhere else; they appear and disappear in pairs, and the total is zero over one joined surface.
The rules of the two phases are their constants and do not change from one generation to the next.

### 8. Black holes curl space back, and each one births many daughters.

Gravity is carried on the edges, which are square edges in the slab and cube edges in open space, so it acts in
two and three dimensions; light travels the body diagonals, which need a cube, so it exists only in three. Gravity
arrives with moving matter: a link's length is set by its own curl, the lengths relax at no cost while twist and
charge stay fixed, the total length is fixed near matter, and light is delayed passing matter. Space has its own
drive to curve; matter supplies the energy that starts it. Inside the horizon gravity pays a Klein store's extra
mass, and no outside cap on that energy applies there.

Inside the space that forms, black holes are where surplus dark matter is crushed by gravity: the one-sided
loops line up with one handedness into the curled slab, a doughnut of squares with the rod through its hole and
the collar of Klein stores on its inner rim. That doughnut is the next X.

Three regions, defined by movement. Outside the doughnut, inside the horizon, the whirlpools: three-dimensional,
cubes, light moving but not leaving, matter and photons moving, every kind of energy turning to dark matter and
gravity. The doughnut: two-dimensional, squares only, where only dark matter and gravity move and twist and curl
are fixed. The stores on the rim: zero-dimensional, where nothing moves, each intact or not, ones and zeros.
There the projection ends and the fundamental arrangement projects again: the strange loop.

Daughter spaces open while the black hole remains in the parent, and every black hole births many new ones, so
the tree of universes multiplies each generation. A black hole is bigger inside than it looks: each daughter is
the size its mother was at the same time since its own birth.

A turn is one universe's own life, from its burp until the last of its black holes has dissolved; the end is
many black holes, carried apart by the cold, expanding void, each dissolving alone.

A rich daughter, detailed enough for observers to evolve, needs a big, well-fed black hole. The loop is a
fractal: very simple rules make the whole pattern, as z → z² + c makes the Mandelbrot set, and how rich it is
comes from the coupling and the amount of curl.

### 9. Each universe keeps its own clock.

Every universe runs an exact copy of one clock, from its own birth to its own heat death; no clock runs across
universes. Universes of one setting that differ only by a quantum outcome are counted each as a whole.

### 10. The quantum world is versions not yet told apart.

An arrangement's undetectably different versions all stand at once, until an interaction makes them
distinguishable. The versions are the branches of the many-worlds reading; the rule that lets them cancel is
the published one: versions carry amplitudes, and a record in the surroundings keeps them apart.

The inner shell of a black hole is a computer: in place of 0 and 1 its symbols are the allowed sets of curl,
twist and spin under the rules, and the simulation plays out all the options in probabilities.

## Part 2. Current definitions

### X and the burp

- **X** (hers): a rod between two curled slabs of opposite hand (step 1). Its stand-in in the toy: the
  **curled slab**, 4 × L × L with six links, two directions open and one curled tight (Update 47, 58), and in
  two dimensions the **tube**, the 4 × L torus with one direction curled (paper 1). X may begin with one
  direction already open (Update 39). *The toy's hot random phase is never X.*
- **The burp** (hers): the sharp release when the curl gives way. In the toy: the tube waits with no memory,
  then opens in one change, giving off exactly the curling cost 4(λ − 1) per point; the push that starts it is
  12 units at every size (measured, paper 1). What the tube opens into is a tube re-glued six around, not a wide
  sheet (exact, O116). In three directions no push or bath has opened a curled X into one space; with points
  compacting in whole layers the slab cycles, one daughter per curling cost (measured, T71, T74), and no graph
  mechanism for the burp has been found there (STATE, piece 5).
- **The pair tie** (hers, Update 124): for the twin pair only, a point with exactly one of the pair's two curled
  directions open pays one curling cost, so curling two directions costs the same as curling one; nothing is
  released when the first opens and the whole release comes with the second. Looks only. **Flagged for her:**
  Update 133 (27 October) replaced this tie by each curled direction carrying its own cost, 4(λ − 1) on each
  side, "half each", while the claim's sentence (confirmed 1 November) still says two cost the same as one.
  Which stands is hers (`STATE.md`, owed item 8). No earlier tie form is current (`WITHDRAWN.md`, section 2).
- **λ** (hers, Updates 152, 155): open space sits at λ = 1 or slightly below (the published model); the barrier
  above 1 belongs to the inner shell of a black hole, looked at one part at a time. The toy's 1.25 is a
  convenience setting from paper 1, kept so that runs share their tubes; a clock test in open space belongs at
  λ = 1.
- **The zero-total rule** (hers, Updates 105, 108; softened in Update 133): curl and uncurl total zero over one
  joined surface; in the toy, squares equal points. Exact: clean six-link space cannot move under the hard rule
  by any one, two, three or restricted four switches; the hard rule is softened (a steep price on straying).

### The lattice and its two quantities (the three-direction toy)

- **The lattice** (exact): black and white points, six links a point, a link joins black to white. Face
  diagonals (1, 1, 0), length √2; body diagonals (1, 1, 1), √3, black to white; box diagonals (1, 1, 2), √6,
  black to black or white to white. A closed loop of box diagonals is one colour. A step changes colour exactly
  when its coordinate sum is odd: edges and body diagonals change colour; face and box diagonals do not.
- **Curl** (hers; exact in the toy): on a link, c = S_e − 4 (flat space has four squares on every link at six
  links; two at four links). One extra square is one whole curl, 360°. Curl is continuous from −1 to 1
  (3 November), its two signs matter and antimatter at equal size. Only a full curl, a loop, stores energy
  stably. In the four-link toy curl and anti-curl are the black-to-black and white-to-white diagonals of one
  square, held as a state c in [−1, 1] on each square (`graphity.curl_pairs`, Update 156).
- **Twist** (hers): lives on diagonals and only where there is curl: no curl, no twist. Discrete: matter twist
  in signed thirds on the box diagonals (the 1 × 1 × 2 diagonals), dark twist in signed halves on the face
  diagonals. The sign is the colour: black +, white −. In two dimensions twist comes in halves only (the Klein
  bottle is the two-dimensional representation); thirds need three dimensions (Update 156).
- **Light and gravity** (hers, Update 142): gravity travels through the cube edges; light travels through the
  body diagonals, whose length is derived from the cube's edges by geometry with no new constant.

### Particles (the standing loop rules; `docs/design/twist_curl_rules_current.md` is the full sheet)

- **A particle** (hers): a closed loop of box diagonals, or several, with one curl of energy per loop.
- **Mass** (hers, Update 148): counts the looped diagonals, twisted or not; a long diagonal (a curl held along
  one untwisted diagonal longer than 1 × 1 × 2, her gluon) counts one. Mass has parts (twist, spin, charge,
  loop) in their own units; MeV is the measured total.
- **The unit** (hers, Update 139): the closed loop of three white thirds (−⅓, −⅓, −⅓), net twist −1, no
  coil, is the electron: 0.511 MeV, charge −1, spin ½. Exact: one looped diagonal is then 0.170 MeV; proton
  5,508 diagonals, neutron 5,516, charged pion 819, muon 621. Open: whether the electron holds more than three.
- **Charge** (hers): twist carried by spin; a loop's charge is its net twist, a whole number of thirds summed
  round the loop. A charge needs two sides and can only be applied on thirds (31 October); a face diagonal's half
  twist is twist, not charge, so a one-sided face loop has charge 0 and meets no light.
- **Spin** (hers, "precise", Update 146): full spin on half a curl (the photon, spin 1); half spin on a full
  curl (matter, spin ½); spin needs twist present, not net twist, so the neutrino spins. Spin with no twist is a
  second kind of force, probably what holds nucleons together.
- **The coil** (hers, 29 October): the loop's writhe, a shape, not a charge and not a mass.
- **Exact, the four test particles**: electron (−1, ½), neutron (0, ½), photon (0, 1), charged pion (1, 0) all
